Relax · Wind down

Extended exhale

In 4Out 6Time about 90 sec

Use it when
You need to come down a level. After a stressful meeting, before bed, or when your heart's still going after something that's already over.
How
Sit or lie down. Breathe in through your nose for four, then let the breath out slowly for six. If six feels like a strain, make the whole breath shorter and keep the exhale a little longer than the inhale.
Notice
When it finishes, let your breath go back to normal. Is your body heavier on the chair? Is your jaw looser? Or nothing much? Any of those answers is useful.

Why the longer exhale?

Your heart rate tends to rise slightly as you breathe in and fall as you breathe out. Making the exhale longer gives more of each breath to the slowing part. Plenty of people find that settling. Some don't, and that's worth knowing too.

Relax · Find a rhythm

Coherent breathing

In 5Out 5Time about 90 sec

Use it when
Your body feels switched on and your thoughts are all over the place. The even count gives your attention something to hold.
How
Follow the circle. Five seconds in, five seconds out, through the nose if you can. Keep it light. You don't need to fill your lungs, just stay with the pace.
Notice
Take one normal breath at the end. Does it feel any different from the breath you took before you started?

Why five and five?

That's about six breaths a minute, often called coherent or resonant breathing. At around that pace, heart rate and breathing tend to rise and fall together in a big, regular wave. The research is promising but still fairly small, so treat it as something to test on yourself.
