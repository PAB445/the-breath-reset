About

I came to breathwork for myself first.

I'm Suraj. I run The Breath Reset in Sydney. Here's how it started, and how I teach.

I've had anxiety since my late teens. Most mornings I'd wake up with a feeling in the pit of my stomach.

From the outside, I was coping. I went to work, saw friends, went to the gym. Inside I often felt numb and disconnected, like my body was bracing for something, even when nothing was actually wrong.

Over time I learned to manage it. Therapy, books and meditation all helped. I got better at noticing my thought loops and stopping myself from getting pulled into them. Honestly, that changed my life.

But even then, my body still felt like it was constantly on edge. My mind understood what was going on. My body didn't seem to have got the message.

"What am I thinking?" "What's happening in my body?"

Three years ago I did my first breathwork session. I remember leaving with a sense of relief. I already understood my anxiety pretty well. This was the first time it felt like my body was starting to soften too.

Everything I'd tried before worked on the thinking. Breathwork worked on the body directly. Since then it's become an essential part of my life, and The Breath Reset is my way of teaching what I've learned: practically, without the mysticism, and at whatever depth you're ready for.

How I teach

What a session with me is like.

I'll explain what we're doing.

You'll know why we're breathing a certain way before we do it. If something is still a theory rather than settled science, I'll say so.

We experiment, then notice.

Most sessions include a short test. Breathe one way, then another, and compare. Your own experience counts for more than anything I tell you.

It doesn't have to be quiet.

Some sessions are slow and still. Others have fast breathing, movement and loud music. Both have their place.

You set the depth.

You can slow down, open your eyes or go back to normal breathing at any point. I'd much rather you notice something small than push for something big.
