Learn to work with your breath, not just take a deep one.

Your breath changes with stress, movement, sleep, emotion and attention. The Breath Reset teaches you how to use it on purpose: to settle your system, to breathe better day to day, and to explore deeper breathwork when you're ready.

The idea

There is no single breath for every state.

Most breathing advice comes down to "slow down and take a deep breath". Sometimes that helps. A lot of the time it's the wrong tool. A wired body, a scattered mind and a hard training session all need something different.

The method

Three ways to work with your breath.

Each one asks a different question of your breath. Most people end up using all three, sometimes in the same week.

01

Relax

Change your state.

Simple tools for when you feel wired, overwhelmed, restless or can't switch off. Slower rhythms and longer exhales you can use on the train, at your desk or lying in bed.

Coherent breathing · Longer exhales · Slow breathing · Sleep

02

Retrain

Change the way you breathe.

Most of your breathing happens when you're not thinking about it. Retrain looks at those habits: nose or mouth, chest or diaphragm, how much air you move, and how you handle a build-up of CO₂.

Nasal breathing · Breath holds · Breathing volume · Breathing during exercise

03

Release

See what happens when you go deeper.

Longer sessions with fuller breathing, music, movement and sound. It can get intense. You decide how far to take it, and every session ends with stillness and time to land.

Connected breathing · Movement · Music · Sound · Integration

You don't graduate from one to the next.

Some days you need Relax. Some days you need Retrain. Sometimes you're ready to Release.

Being regulated doesn't mean being calm all the time. It means you can move between states. You can feel activation, emotion or intensity without needing it gone straight away, and you can bring yourself back down when you choose to.

Field notes

Four things worth knowing about your breath.

CO₂

CO₂ isn't just waste.

The level of CO₂ in your blood is one of the main signals your brain uses to decide when you breathe. It also affects how easily oxygen is released to your tissues, which is known as the Bohr effect. Breathe far more than you need and CO₂ drops, which is part of why over-breathing can leave you light-headed or tingly.

Pace

Slower isn't always better.

Breathing at around six breaths a minute settles a lot of people. It's one tool. Faster, fuller breathing raises activation on purpose, and learning to stay with that is a skill too.

Nose

Why the nose?

Your nose warms, humidifies and filters air before it reaches your lungs. It also adds a little resistance, which tends to slow breathing down and keep the volume closer to what your body needs.

Breath holds

What a breath hold tests.

A relaxed hold after a normal exhale gives a rough idea of how strongly you react to rising CO₂. It shifts with sleep, stress, time of day and what you've eaten. Treat it as a reference point to track, not a score or a diagnosis.

Breath Lab

Don't take my word for it. Try your breath.

Short experiments you can do right now, sitting wherever you are. You don't have to believe anything first. Try one, then notice what changed. Sometimes nothing will, and that's worth knowing too.

Why I do this

My mind understood. My body didn't.

I've had anxiety since my late teens. From the outside I was fine. I went to work, saw friends, went to the gym. Inside, my body felt like it was bracing for something, even when nothing was wrong.

Therapy, books and meditation all helped. I got better at noticing my thought loops and not getting pulled into them, and honestly, that changed my life. But my body was still on edge.

"What am I thinking?" "What's happening in my body?"

Three years ago I did my first breathwork session. I remember leaving with a sense of relief. I already understood my anxiety. This was the first time my body seemed to soften with it. The Breath Reset grew out of that.

How sessions work

You're in charge of your breath.

Breathwork can be gentle or strong. The same rules apply either way.

You can always stop

Go back to normal breathing, slow down, open your eyes, look around the room or stop completely. You never need to explain why.

Deeper work needs a check first

Before any Release session I'll ask about your health. Faster, fuller breathing isn't right for everyone, including during pregnancy or with epilepsy, heart conditions, high blood pressure, recent surgery or some mental health conditions. If that's you, we'll stick to slower work, or you can check with your doctor first.

Intensity isn't the point

Some people get emotional. Some feel calm. Some feel very little. All of that is fine, and nobody is chasing a breakthrough.

It isn't therapy

Breathwork doesn't replace medical or mental health care. Plenty of people use it alongside both.
